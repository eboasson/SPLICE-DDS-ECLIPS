I have worked, with some interruptions, for about thirty years on a niche technology that is now best known as “DDS”, the “Data Distribution System” as specified by the Object Management Group. This is a key part of the infrastructure of modern robotics, air-traffic management, combat management systems, railway networks, automotive and many other systems. It even went to space. For such an important piece of infrastructure, there is surprisingly little easily accessible information on the early history and the idea behind it.

This year marks the fortieth anniversary of the first public presentations of the model underpinning DDS, and this time of year moreover marks the anniversary of the passing of my father, who proposed it more than forty years ago. This is a good occasion to record the history—and to go into the ideas behind it that seem to have been forgotten along the way.
